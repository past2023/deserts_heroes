# Desert’s Heroes — World and Story Bible

Updated: 2026-07-23

## High concept

Across a retro-futurist galaxy, entire civilizations are being forced backward by the **Atavist Dominion**, an alliance of war machines, cyborg priesthoods, predatory aliens, armored humanoid regimes and autonomous fleets. The Dominion destroys schools, observatories, archives, power grids and medical systems so that conquered planets become isolated, fearful and easy to rule. Its promise is simple: erase modern knowledge and return the universe to a permanent Stone Age.

Opposing it are the **Desert’s Heroes**, explorer-soldiers of the **Scientific Frontier Corps**. They are field scientists, engineers, medics, historians, pilots and combat specialists. Their first duty is to observe and understand. Their second is to share science only with consent and care. Their final duty is to defend inhabited worlds when the Dominion attacks.

The Corps does not conquer planets or replace local cultures. It protects each world’s right to choose its own future.

## Tone and visual identity

The setting combines:

- optimistic 1950s planetary adventure serials;
- painted 1970s science-fiction concept art;
- industrial 1980s spacecraft and military machinery;
- arcade run-and-gun energy;
- warm desert archaeology and cold orbital mystery;
- serious stakes with bold, readable heroic action.

Technology looks repaired, modular and physical. Computers have glowing tubes and chunky switches. Spacecraft carry scientific instruments beside oversized weapons. Ancient ruins coexist with reactors, radio towers and dimensional portals.

## Historical background

### The First Illumination

Centuries ago, independent worlds exchanged medicine, mathematics, astronomy and engineering through the Star Road. Knowledge moved freely, but no single empire controlled it.

### The Great Silence

The Star Road collapsed after coordinated attacks on navigation beacons and archives. Many planets lost contact. Some recovered; others entered centuries of technological decline.

### Rise of the Atavist Dominion

The Dominion emerged from the silence. Its factions disagree about species, religion and government, but share one doctrine: knowledge creates resistance. They use robots where life is expendable, cyborgs where obedience is required, aliens where terror is useful, humanoid armies where occupation must look legitimate, and massive vehicles where history itself must be buried.

### The Scientific Frontier Corps

Surviving universities, free planets and nomadic laboratories formed the Corps. Its expeditions restore communications, investigate lost technology, defend civilian infrastructure and reconnect isolated worlds without erasing local identity.

## The heroes

### Juan P. — Desert Vanguard

Veteran expedition leader, engineer and field historian. Juan believes every ruin is a message from people who refused to disappear. Balanced and reliable, he carries full equipment capacity.

### Elena K. — Star Scout

Astrophysicist, pilot and reconnaissance specialist. Elena is the fastest explorer and the first to enter unstable portals. She values evidence over military certainty.

### Sergio H. — Iron Nomad

Systems mechanic, rescue specialist and armored field scientist. Sergio protects teams in environments where one mistake would be fatal. He moves more slowly but can survive an additional impact.

## Enemy families

- **Ash Robots:** abandoned or repurposed machines programmed to burn archives and infrastructure.
- **Cyborg Enforcers:** living soldiers rebuilt around Dominion control systems.
- **Nullborn Aliens:** organisms adapted to dimensional fractures and hostile to conventional technology.
- **Humanoid Regressors:** armies that benefit politically from keeping populations uneducated.
- **Atavist Vehicles:** modular tanks, helicopters, fortress engines and orbital warships designed to demolish modern infrastructure.
- **Archive Hunters:** elite units that steal scientific artifacts before destroying all local records.

## Mission 01 — Dune Frontier

The Corps detects a surviving Star Road signal beneath a desert planet. The Dominion is attacking observatories, radio bunkers and archaeological sites while preparing an armored fortress to erase the region permanently.

The expedition must:

1. land beyond the occupation perimeter;
2. rescue captured researchers;
3. recover weapons and scientific supplies hidden in local structures;
4. cross lava fractures and unstable industrial platforms;
5. investigate a time-rift portal;
6. destroy the armored fortress;
7. preserve the signal that points toward future planets.

The orbital time-rift is not a separate conquest. It is a fractured research corridor containing machines, transferable equipment and evidence that the Great Silence was engineered.

## Campaign worlds

- **Tutorial — Frontier Training Annex:** movement, weapons, rescue ethics and field-science procedures.
- **Mission 01 — Dune Frontier:** archaeology, occupation machinery, lava fractures and the first Star Road signal.
- **Mission 02 — Ember Citadel:** volcanic industry and a society forced to power Dominion factories.
- **Mission 03 — Silent Orbit:** abandoned stations, dimensional organisms and missing research crews.
- **Mission 04 — Lost Sands:** ancient lunar archives hidden beneath a dead world.
- **Mission 05 — Verdant Siege:** a living biosphere resisting mechanical extraction.
- **Mission 06 — Frozen Signal:** the source of a transmission connected to the Great Silence.

## Narrative rules

1. The heroes are explorers and scientists before they are soldiers.
2. Science is presented as cooperation, curiosity, medicine, communication and shared evidence—not cultural domination.
3. Enemies oppose education and independent thought; they are not defined as evil because of species.
4. Every planet should contain local people, history and agency.
5. Combat protects civilians, knowledge or ecological survival.
6. Boss dialogue should reveal ideology, not only threaten the player.
7. Hero dialogue should mix observation, empathy, tactical clarity and wonder.
8. Humor may relieve tension but should not trivialize planetary destruction.

## Dialogue presentation

Gameplay dialogue uses a compact portrait transmission with the speaker’s emotional face, localized name and teleprompter text. Scanlines, chromatic separation, jitter and dropout make every message feel like unstable field video. Dialogue never pauses simulation, but important lines are queued so they do not overlap. Bosses, elite enemies and heroes can speak. Rescued prisoners use modular authored bodies and represent researchers, engineers and local witnesses rather than anonymous rewards.

## Core theme

**A civilization is not saved by giving it a future. It is saved by defending its right to build one.**
