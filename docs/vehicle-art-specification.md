# Desert's Heroes — vehicle PNG and animation specification

## Purpose

This document defines the production contract for replacing the generated helicopters, tanks, allied assault vehicle and final fortress with direct transparent pixel-art PNG animation.

The target is the dense, expressive mechanical animation quality associated with classic 1990s arcade run-and-gun games, while keeping every design, marking, silhouette and frame original to **Desert's Heroes**. Do not trace, edit or reproduce Metal Slug sprites.

## Global game scale

```text
Logical game viewport: 960 × 540
Gameplay ground line: Y 470
Main character frame: 100 × 100
Main character aim-up frame: 100 × 123
Rendering: native-size nearest-neighbour Canvas pixels
Source direction: every vehicle faces RIGHT
```

Vehicles will be displayed at native source size. Do not create 2× or 4× enlarged files unless requested; one source pixel should equal one game logical pixel.

## Required source canvases

| Vehicle | Frame canvas | Pivot / anchor | Recommended damageable hitbox | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Allied rocket/assault tank | **180 × 120** | `(90,116)` bottom-center | `140 × 82` | Player vehicle |
| Standard enemy tank | **200 × 120** | `(100,116)` bottom-center | `170 × 88` | Regular armored enemy |
| Standard helicopter | **240 × 140** | `(120,75)` center | `190 × 72` body only | Flying enemy |
| Gunship helicopter | **360 × 200** | `(180,104)` center | `300 × 118` body only | Mid-level miniboss |
| Final armored fortress | **440 × 260** | `(220,252)` bottom-center | `390 × 218` | Final boss |

The transparent canvas size must remain identical across every state and frame belonging to that vehicle. Never crop frames tightly around individual poses.

### Why these sizes

The player is 100 px tall. A standard tank should be about 1.7–2 player widths, a helicopter about 2.2 player widths, the gunship about one-third of the viewport, and the final fortress slightly under half of the 960 px viewport. This gives readable arcade silhouettes without hiding the entire battlefield.

## Pivot and baseline rules

### Ground vehicles

- Pivot is the listed bottom-center coordinate.
- Tracks/wheels must touch the same baseline in every idle, movement, recoil and damage frame.
- No frame may move the entire vehicle inside its canvas to simulate movement; world movement is handled by code.
- Recoil may move the body/turret relative to the fixed tracks and pivot.

### Helicopters

- Pivot is a stable body center, not the center of the full rotor silhouette.
- Rotor animation must not move the fuselage pivot.
- Body bob is handled by the engine; do not move the full helicopter vertically between hover frames.
- Rotor blades, tail rotor and landing gear do not belong to the damage hitbox.

## Do not bake these effects into vehicle PNGs

The engine creates these internally so they can use hit-stop, lighting, timing and color consistently:

- muzzle flashes;
- bullets, shells, rockets and bombs;
- rocket exhaust and smoke trails;
- impact sparks and hit rings;
- explosion fireballs and shockwaves;
- screen flashes;
- ground contact shadows;
- damage flash/white silhouette;
- persistent smoke from low health;
- text, numbers, faction labels and HUD elements.

Vehicle art should include physical recoil, opening hatches, broken armor and mechanical motion, but not the temporary FX listed above.

# Pixel-art direction

## Silhouette

- The vehicle must remain recognizable as a solid black silhouette.
- Exaggerate the main cannon, rotor, treads, bomb bay and boss weak points.
- Avoid a perfectly rectangular body; use stepped armor, asymmetry and readable protrusions.
- Keep designs original. Do not copy Metal Slug vehicle silhouettes, emblems or proportions exactly.

## Palette

Recommended limits per vehicle:

```text
Standard vehicle: 24–40 colors
Gunship:          32–48 colors
Final boss:       40–64 colors
```

Use deliberate color ramps rather than unrestricted noise:

- 1 near-black outline color, not pure black everywhere;
- 3–5 metal body shades;
- 2–4 warm highlight shades;
- 2–4 cooler shadow shades;
- 2–3 rust/dirt shades;
- 2–4 faction accent colors;
- small high-value specular pixels.

Suggested lighting direction: **upper-left/front**. Top surfaces should be warmer and brighter; undersides, tread cavities and rotor undersides should be cooler and darker.

## Materials and detail

Use clustered details with purpose:

- rivets in short patterns rather than on every edge;
- chipped paint concentrated on corners and moving joints;
- dark grease around hinges, tracks and exhausts;
- sand dust near lower surfaces;
- bright highlights on barrel rims, rotor edges and armor bevels;
- readable vents, bolts and seams at gameplay scale;
- dents and replacement plates to add asymmetry.

Avoid single-pixel random noise spread evenly across the whole sprite. Detail density should support the form, not flatten it.

## Outline and edges

- Use a 1–2 px near-black outer contour.
- Break the outline with colored highlights on strongly lit upper edges.
- Use selective inner outlines, especially around moving mechanical parts.
- No anti-aliased edge pixels.
- Alpha should normally be either 0 or 255.
- Do not leave a white or colored matte around transparency.

## Animation principles

- Animate mass: large bodies move less than barrels, suspension, antennae and loose panels.
- Use one clear anticipation frame before heavy cannon fire.
- Heavy recoil should happen in one fast frame and recover over 3–5 frames.
- Tracks must cycle without the hull sliding inside the canvas.
- Helicopter rotor timing should read at both 60 Hz and lower mobile refresh rates.
- Damage animation should become less regular as HP decreases.
- Destruction should progress from local failure to structural collapse.
- Preserve pivot and socket positions throughout every animation.

# File and delivery rules

## Naming

Use lowercase snake-case names exactly as specified above.

## Sheet layout

- Horizontal sheets are read left to right.
- Multi-row grids are read left to right, then top to bottom.
- No spacing between frame cells.
- No duplicated padding between cells.
- Every cell has the exact vehicle canvas size.
- Keep each PNG dimension at or below 4096 px where practical.

## PNG format

```text
Format: PNG
Color: RGBA or indexed PNG with valid transparency
Background: fully transparent
Filtering: none
Anti-aliasing: none
Direction: facing right
Color profile: sRGB preferred
```

## Delivery package

Recommended ZIP structure:

```text
vehicles/
  ally_tank/
  enemy_tank/
  helicopter/
  gunship/
  boss_fortress/
  vehicle-notes.txt
```

In `vehicle-notes.txt`, list:

- any changed pivot;
- final socket coordinates;
- intended frame rate;
- frame that releases a bomb/shell;
- frame where recoil peaks;
- frame where a hatch is fully open;
- any intentional semi-transparent pixels.

# Final QA checklist

Before delivery, confirm:

- [ ] Every frame has the exact required canvas size.
- [ ] All source art faces right.
- [ ] Ground vehicle tracks stay on the baseline.
- [ ] Helicopter body pivot does not jitter.
- [ ] Rotor extremities never leave the frame.
- [ ] Muzzle sockets stay aligned during recoil.
- [ ] No muzzle flashes, bullets, smoke, shadows or explosions are baked in.
- [ ] Alpha edges have no white halo.
- [ ] Frame sheets do not exceed agreed grid dimensions.
- [ ] Animation loops have no visible position jump.
- [ ] Damageable body remains readable behind effects.
- [ ] Designs and insignia are original to Desert's Heroes.
