# Mission intro, expanded audio and discrete HUD

## Rocket-board mission entrance

Every new Arcade or Survival run begins with a short real-time sequence generated by the existing engine:

1. A twin-engine flying surfboard enters from the left.
2. The board decelerates with an ease-out curve and hovers at the deployment point.
3. The hero rides in the normal idle pose while both engines emit animated exhaust. The foot anchor now uses the exact animated deck-top equation (`boardY + hoverBob - 6`), eliminating the previous floating gap.
4. The hero jumps from the board and falls under game gravity.
5. Landing creates dust, sparks, a small camera impulse and a start cue.
6. Normal spawns, timers and HUD begin only after the landing.
7. The board continues accelerating upward/right and exits while gameplay is already active.

The board is drawn procedurally by `Sprites.drawRocketBoard`; it uses no new image asset. It includes an armored deck, pointed nose, stabilizer fins, rear rocket pods, twin additive exhaust cones, smoke and sparks.

During the cinematic:

- player input and encounter spawning are locked;
- gameplay HUD is hidden;
- the mission banner timer is held at zero;
- the soundtrack can begin under the arrival;
- pause and main-menu navigation remain available.

## Expanded procedural SFX

The WebAudio layer now has dedicated cues for:

- board flyby/rocket engine;
- jump release and landing;
- mission-start confirmation;
- alternating footsteps;
- brass casing impacts with rate limiting;
- ammo-depleted click;
- light/energy hit confirmation with rate limiting;
- enemy rifle, turret, helicopter, gunship and boss shot identities;
- guided-missile target lock;
- boss phase transition.

Existing weapon, explosion, pickup, vehicle, death and heaven cues remain. Rate limiting prevents machine-gun fire from creating excessive audio nodes.

## Two-track soundtrack

The former intensity variations are now two structurally different procedural compositions.

### Gameplay track

- 118 ms sequencer step;
- warm desert-action bass phrase;
- melodic 32-step lead;
- sparse arpeggio at action intensity;
- kick, snare and restrained hi-hat layers.

### Boss track

- 94 ms sequencer step;
- darker lower bass pattern;
- denser chromatic lead phrase;
- more aggressive kick/snare grid;
- continuous high percussion and arpeggio;
- dedicated transition sting when the boss track starts.

`SFX.setIntensity(2)` selects the boss track. Levels 0–1 remain on the gameplay composition. Survival no longer activates boss music simply because the wave number is high.

## Smaller, more discrete HUD

The previous opaque beveled HUD was replaced with a corner-based hierarchy:

- panel fill alpha is approximately 20–34% instead of near-black opacity;
- borders are one-pixel translucent weapon colors with small white corner marks;
- a 44×38 portrait/lives chip sits at the top-left;
- the active weapon occupies a 211×38 chip beside the portrait;
- secondary ammunition occupies a 199×34 top-right chip;
- score moved to a low-priority 170×20 lower-left strip;
- the old persistent bottom weapon deck was removed completely;
- boss health is a thin bottom strip and only appears during the fight;
- low special-weapon ammunition pulses red without adding another label;
- weapon and secondary pickups generate a short expanding outline around only the relevant chip;
- the God Mode badge is a small translucent green marker;
- the cinematic hides all gameplay HUD until landing.

This layout follows visual hierarchy: weapon and secondary state are quick corner reads, score is visually quiet, and boss health becomes dominant only when relevant.
